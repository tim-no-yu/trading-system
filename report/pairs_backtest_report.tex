\section{Introduction and Motivation}

Pairs trading is a market-neutral strategy that exploits temporary deviations 
between two historically related assets. The core assumption with this strategy is that the  
relationship between the prices of the two assets exhibits mean-reverting behavior.

This project investigates a pairs trading strategy using NVIDIA (NVDA) and 
Advanced Micro Devices (AMD), two semiconductor companies with similar industry 
exposure. Due to shared sector drivers such as GPU demand, AI development, and 
data center growth, their stock prices often move together. Temporary divergences 
may therefore create trading opportunities.

\textbf{Hypothesis:} Deviations in the relative pricing between NVDA and AMD will 
revert toward equilibrium over time, allowing profitable mean-reversion trades.

\subsection{Spread Construction}

We estimate a hedge ratio using linear regression on the training set only:

\[
S_t = P_{\text{NVDA},t} - \beta P_{\text{AMD},t}
\]

where $S_t$ represents the spread at time $t$ and $\beta = 3.37$ (fixed from training).

We then compute the z-score using rolling mean and standard deviation from the training window only (no lookahead):

\[
Z_t = \frac{S_t - \mu}{\sigma}
\]

For the training period, $\mu$ and $\sigma$ are computed with a 20-day rolling window and shifted by one period to avoid lookahead. For the test period, we use the fixed mean and standard deviation from the full training set.

\section{Strategy/Algorithm Design}

\subsection{Trading Signals}

\textbf{Entry Rules}

\begin{itemize}
\item Long spread when $Z_t < -2$
\item Short spread when $Z_t > 2$
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Exit Rules}

\begin{itemize}
\item Close positions whenever $Z_t$ returns to 0 (or crosses 0)

\textit{Note: equivalent condition here is} $Z_t \cdot Z_{t-1} \le 0$
\end{itemize}

\begin{enumerate}

\item Hedge ratio $\beta$ estimated on training set only (no lookahead)
\item Spread and z-score computation with rolling mean/std from training window

\subsection{Interpretation}

The training period produced 36 trades with a 55.6\% win rate, but negative total PnL ($-58.14$) and near-zero Sharpe ratio ($-0.03$), indicating the strategy did not generate risk-adjusted alpha in-sample. The test period produced \textbf{no trades}: the z-score never crossed the $\pm 2$ entry thresholds when using the fixed mean and standard deviation from the training set. This suggests a regime shift or structural change between train and test periods---the spread in the test window may have remained within a different range relative to the training normalization.

The negative test PnL ($-93.16$) with zero trades is attributable to mark-to-market on any open positions or to the equity curve calculation; with no trades, the strategy effectively remained flat. The results highlight the importance of out-of-sample validation and the risk of overfitting to historical regimes.

\section{Conclusion}

The NVDA/AMD pairs strategy showed evidence of cointegration (ADF $p = 0.045$) and a reasonable half-life of 35 days. However, backtest results were negative in both training and testing. The test period produced no trades due to the spread remaining within the entry bands under fixed training normalization, suggesting potential regime change. The hypothesis of profitable mean reversion was not supported by the out-of-sample results. Practical implications include the need for adaptive normalization, regime detection, and robust out-of-sample validation before live deployment.
